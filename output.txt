Abstract—The architecture of commercial recommendation
systems is undergoing a paradigm shift. The traditional ’Retrieveand-Rank’ pipeline involves scoring millions of item embeddings.
It is being replaced by Generative Recommendation (GR). GR
frameworks unify discovery as a sequence-to-sequence generation task and leverage the auto-regressive capabilities of Large
Language Models (LLMs). The efficacy of GR however, is
bottlenecked by its indexing strategy (how items are encoded
into discrete, generative tokens). Current state-of-the-art systems
use Semantic Identifiers (SIDs). SIDs are derived from Residual
Quantized Variational Autoencoders (RQ-VAE) that operate on
textual metadata. Therefore, in visual-heavy domains like shortform video and e-commerce, this unimodal reliance creates a
’Semantic-Visual Gap’. However, recent studies highlight severe
limitations in current multimodal SID extensions, specifically
’modality sensitivity collapse’ and the ’semantic-behavioral gap’.
Which might result in similar identifiers for visually distinct
items. To tackle these problems, we propose the MultimodalAugmented Semantic ID (MASI) framework. MASI abandons
traditional early-fusion techniques in favor of a modality-specific
Late-Fusion Codebook strategy, coupled with a Behavior-Aware
Contrastive alignment phase. We aim to improve zero-shot, coldstart discovery by encoding both aligned semantic categorization
and visual features into a unified generative token space.
Index Terms—Generative Recommendation, SASRec, Semantic Identifiers, Multimodal Fusion, Cold-Start Discovery, RQVAE, Generative Retrieval.

I. I NTRODUCTION
Organizations like Meta, Amazon, Apple, Netflix, Google,
and OpenAI process billions of interactions daily. Traditionally, these platforms operated on a multi-stage discriminative
pipeline:
1) A fast retrieval model selects a few hundred candidates.
2) A heavy ranking model scores them.
However, the reasoning and generative capabilities of Large
Language Models (LLMs) have catalyzed a shift towards
Generative Recommendation (GR) [Liang and Y. Zhang 2025].
In GR, recommendation is not a classification or scoring
problem. It is a conditional text generation task. Given a user’s
chronological interaction history, the model auto-regressively
”writes” the identifier of the next item [Yang, H. Lin, Z. Zhang,
et al. 2025]. The critical factor in this paradigm is the indexing
strategy. Traditional systems used atomic IDs (e.g., assigning
the integer ID_1051 to a specific movie). These IDs are
semantically hollow. When a new item is introduced (the

cold-start problem), its atomic ID has no learned embedding,
causing the system to fail.
To solve this, researchers introduced Semantic Identifiers
(SIDs). SIDs are hierarchical codewords derived from item
content (e.g., mapping a movie to a token sequence like [14,
102, 12]) [Singh et al. 2024].
SIDs however, are currently generated almost exclusively
from textual descriptions. We argue that this creates a fundamental flaw in domains where visual features dictate preference. This proposal introduces the MASI framework, which is
designed to encode both text and images into a single, unified
generative token space.

This section provides a breakdown of the current state-ofthe-art to understand the necessity of multimodal SIDs.
A. The Attention mechanism: Self-Attentive Sequential Rec
(SASRec)
The foundational pillar of personalization before GR, was
the modeling of sequential user behavior. Early approaches
used Markov Chains, but the breakthrough was the SelfAttentive Sequential Recommendation (SASRec) model [Kang
and McAuley 2018].
SASRec deploys the Transformer architecture for recommendation. Instead of words in a sentence, the input is represented as a sequence of item embeddings E = [e1 , e2 , . . . , en ].
The item embeddings represent a user’s chronological clicks.
SASRec employs multi-head self-attention to dynamically
weigh historical interactions, allowing the model to capture
both long-term preferences and short-term interactions without
the rigid constraints posed by a Markov window. The attention
mechanism computes a weighted sum of past items:

recommended (causing the cold-start problem).
B. The LLM Shift: Recommendation as Language Processing
To address the scaling and flexibility issues of SASRec,
researchers proposed treating recommendation as a Natural
Language Processing (NLP) task. The landmark P5 framework
[Geng et al. 2022] demonstrated that recommendation could
be unified within a standard encoder-decoder LLM (like T5).
In P5, the input is modeled as a natural language prompt
template: ”User A watched ’Breaking Bad’, ’Better Call Saul’.
What will they watch next?” The model outputs a text string:
”Narcos”.
The Flaw: While conceptually elegant, P5 suffered from
severe hallucination. Standard LLMs use sub-word tokenization (e.g., Byte-Pair Encoding). If an item is named ”iPhone
15 Plus”, the LLM might generate ”iPhone 15 Pro” if the
probabilistic token for ”Pro” was slightly higher. Generating
raw text strings is too unconstrained for precise item retrieval.
C. Hallucination’s Solution: Generative Retrieval & Semantic
IDs
To fix hallucination P5 suffered from, the community moved
to Generative Retrieval, popularized by the TIGER framework
[Rajput et al. 2023]. The model generates discrete, abstract tokens called ’Semantic Identifiers’ (SIDs), instead of generating
English text.
Each SID is structured as a hierarchical tuple, e.g., Si =
⟨c1 , c2 , c3 ⟩. The hierarchy is strictly enforced: c1 might represent a broad cluster (Electronics), c2 a sub-cluster (mobile phones), and c3 the specific item (Apple iPhone 15).
The auto-regressive model is then trained on these discrete
tokens, thereby reducing the generation space from O(|I|)
to O(D × |V|), where D is the tuple length and |V| is
the small vocabulary size of the codebook. If the model
generates a prefix ⟨c1 , c2 ⟩, it means that it has successfully
narrowed the search space to a specific semantic cluster,
thereby solving the hallucination problem and allowing for
zero-shot recommendation of new items that share that prefix.
D. The Mathematical Engine: RQ-VAE
The current standard for generating SIDs is the Residual
Quantized Variational Autoencoder (RQ-VAE) [Lee et al.
2022]. Unlike standard clustering algorithms (like K-Means),
RQ-VAE learns a structured hierarchy of embeddings.
Given a continuous text embedding of an item z ∈ Rd
(extracted via BERT), the RQ-VAE uses D sequential codebooks C = {C1 , . . . , CD }, where each codebook contains K
trainable vectors.
1) Level 1: The model finds the closest vector to z in C1 .
Let its index be c1 .
2) Compute Residual: The model subtracts the codebook
vector from z to find the ”error” or residual: r1 = z −
C1 (c1 ).

3) Level 2: The model then quantizes the residual r1 using
the second codebook C2 , yielding index c2 .
This process repeats D times. The final SID is the sequence
of indices (c1 , c2 , . . . , cD ). Mathematically, this forces a tree
structure:
• c1 captures the largest variance (broad category).
• cD captures the smallest residual nuances (fine-grained
item details).
E. The Visual Gap and Multimodal Encoders
The biggest shortcoming of current SID framework models
like TIGER is their exclusive reliance on text. They only apply
RQ-VAE to text embeddings. When dealing with highly visual
categories (like fashion, home decor, or short-form video) textual descriptions are often incredibly sparse or completely
identical for items that actually look different.
To bridge this visual gap, we look at foundational visuallanguage models like CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining) [Radford et al. 2021]. CLIP is trained with an
InfoNCE contrastive loss, which mathematically forces the image’s embedding to be highly cosine-similar to the embedding
of its text description, and dissimilar to unrelated texts. CLIP
provides a unified latent space where visual features can be
mathematically quantified alongside text descriptions.
F. Recent Advances: 2025-2026 SOTA
State-of-the-art research has identified several bottlenecks
in early GR models, leading to a wave of new advanced
frameworks:
• Multimodal Fusion (CEMG): The CEMG framework
addresses the superficial integration of collaborative signals by using them as queries to dynamically weigh visual
and textual features during tokenization [Y. Lin et al.
2026].
• Differentiable Indexing (DIGER): To address the ”objective mismatch” where SIDs are optimized for reconstruction rather than recommendation, DIGER implements Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization that allows
recommendation gradients to flow back to the codebook
[Fu et al. 2026].
• Long ID Potential (ACERec): ACERec decouples tokenization granularity from computational budget using
an ”Attentive Token Merger,” allowing for expressive 32token IDs while also maintaining efficient inference [Xia
et al. 2026].
III. P ROBLEM F ORMULATION AND G AP A NALYSIS
While foundational visual-language models like CLIP [Radford et al. 2021] bridge the visual-text gap, applying RQ-VAE
to multimodal embeddings poses two critical limitations.
A. Limitation 1: Modality Sensitivity and Early Fusion Collapse
The intuitive approach to MGR is ”Early Fusion”: concatenating text and image embeddings before the RQ-VAE.
Zhu et al. 2025 proved a flaw ’modality sensitivity’. During

early fusion, one modality could mathematically dominate the
representation space. Even recent dynamic weighing models
like CEMG [Y. Lin et al. 2026] still operate within an earlyfusion architecture, leaving them fundamentally vulnerable to
cross-modal noise and indexing collisions for visually distinct
items sharing similar text.
B. Limitation 2: The Semantic-Behavioral Gap
Xu et al. 2025 identified the ’Semantic-Behavioral Gap’.
An RQ-VAE trained solely on content assumes that items
with similar aesthetics should receive similar SIDs. However,
a designer black t-shirt and a budget black t-shirt, although
visually identical, appeal and cater to entirely different user
demographics. While advanced models like DIGER [Fu et al.
2026] attempt to bridge this by end-to-end differentiable
indexing, their Gumbel-Softmax routing is extremely unstable
and complex to train. A more modular and stable pipeline
is required to align semantic generation with actual user
preferences.
IV. P ROPOSED M ETHODOLOGY: MASI
To resolve these bottlenecks, we propose the MultimodalAugmented Semantic ID (MASI) framework. MASI consists
of three distinct phases that are designed to maximize modality
independence and behavioral alignment.
A. Phase 1: Behavior-Aware Contrastive Alignment
To solve the Semantic-Behavioral Gap (Limitation 2), our
representations must respect collaborative user signals. However, if we mix the modalities while learning these signals,
we risk modality interference, where the model ignores visual
nuances in favor of text. To prevent this, we isolate the
training. We freeze the base CLIP encoders so they do not
suffer from catastrophic forgetting, and we attach independent,
learnable projection heads to each modality. This allows each
modality to learn user preferences independently. For an item

space without needing any prior clicks.

features often overpower visual features), we process the
aligned text and image embeddings completely separately. By
maintaining two entirely independent RQ-VAE pipelines, we
ensure that the visual characteristics are matched to a dedicated
visual codebook (C vis ), while the text characteristics are
matched to a dedicated text codebook (C txt ). This guarantees
that neither modality loses its unique information. For a given

C. Phase 3: Recommender-Side Cross-Modal Alignment
As demonstrated by Zhu et al. 2025, utilizing separate codebooks does not inherently equip an autoregressive sequence
model to comprehend cross-modal correlations. To bridge this,
we introduce an auxiliary pre-training phase on the exact
Transformer backbone used for downstream recommendations.
Specifically, the model is trained to reconstruct visual tokens
Sivis from textual tokens Sitxt (and vice versa) using a crossmodal Masked Language Modeling (MLM) objective. After
this discrete alignment phase, the Transformer is finally finetuned on chronological user interaction sequences. In essence,
Phase 1 ensures that the dense, continuous vectors respect
user behavior before they enter the codebooks, while Phase
3 ensures that the final, discrete SID tokens are cross-modally
aligned for the autoregressive Transformer. We anticipate that
these two stages operate synergistically (one organizes raw
data and the other organizes the generative vocabulary) though
we will conduct ablation studies to verify that they do not
overlap in utility.

B. Mathematical Soundness of Dual Codebooks
Because we separate the quantization process (with the dualcodebook approach), the mathematical updates (gradients)
for the visual loss (∇LRQ vis ) only ever affect the visual
codebook (C vis ) and embedding (evis ). By enforcing this
strict architectural boundary, we mathematically prevent the
text modality from overpowering the visual nuances during
training. This directly resolves the modality collapse problem
identified in early-fusion models.
VI. E XPERIMENTAL D ESIGN AND I MPLEMENTATION P LAN
A. Dataset Configuration
We will utilize the Amazon Reviews 2023 Dataset Lab
2023, filtering the ”Clothing, Shoes, and Jewelry” subset (5core strategy) to yield ∼100,000 active users and ∼50,000
items with valid high-resolution images.
B. Evaluation Protocol & Baselines
Employing a Leave-One-Out (LOO) protocol, we will partition the test set into Warm-Start and Zero-Shot Cold-Start.
Metrics will include HR@10, NDCG@10, Coverage@10

We will implement the dual-codebook quantization and
contrastive graph sampling by leveraging modern AI-assisted
coding agents (Google Gemini, GitHub Copilot) for rapid
generation of code boilerplates (like PyTorch data-loader boilerplate and for parsing RecBole’s internal abstractions).
• Week 1-2: Amazon 2023 ETL pipelines. Extract CLIP
vectors.
• Week 3-4: Construct bipartite graphs; implement Phase
1 mixed negative sampling and train projection heads.
• Week 5-6: Train Phase 2 dual RQ-VAEs. Implement
Phase 3 MLM pre-training within RecBole.
• Week 7-8: Execute generative fine-tuning, conduct LOO
evaluations/ablations, and finalize the IEEE manuscript.
VII. L IMITATIONS AND F UTURE W ORK
MASI vs. ACERec: While MASI’s late-fusion dualcodebook approach could prove to solve modality collapse, it
doubles the generative sequence length per item (2D tokens).
We treat this as an accepted trade-off for capturing rich
and independent multimodal representations. Future work will
tackle the resulting linear increase in inference latency by
incorporating ACERec’s [Xia et al. 2026] Attentive Token
Merging to compress the S f inal representation prior to decoding, optimizing efficiency without sacrificing multimodal
granularity.
VIII. C ONCLUSION
Generative Recommendation’s efficacy is bound by item
representations. By going through 2025-2026 SOTA literature, we identified that basic multimodal extensions suffer
from modality collapse and behavioral misalignment. The
MASI framework tries to rectify these flaws through Independent Modality Quantization, Behavior-Aware Alignment,

and Cross-Modal Sequence Modeling, ensuring the generative
engine benefits from true visual features and collaborative
utilities to solve cold-start discovery.
